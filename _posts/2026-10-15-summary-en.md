### [AI-designed proteins target bonds stable for centuries](https://arstechnica.com/science/2026/10/researchers-use-ai-to-design-proteins-that-cut-up-other-proteins/) ⭐️ 8.0/10



rss · Ars Technica · Oct 14, 17:10

#### Summary

Researchers have used AI design tools to create proteins capable of cleaving chemical bonds that are normally stable for hundreds of years. This advance demonstrates that AI-driven protein design can produce enzymes with novel catalytic activity targeting exceptionally stable bonds—targets that are resistant to conventional enzymatic degradation. Published by Ars Technica, the work highlights the growing capability of combining AI with computational biology to achieve protein functions not easily found in nature.

#### Background

Peptides are biological molecules composed of amino acids linked together by peptide bonds, which are among the most stable covalent bonds in biochemistry. These bonds resist hydrolysis under normal physiological conditions, meaning they can persist for centuries without breaking down naturally. This exceptional stability is why proteins in ancient specimens can survive for long periods, but it also means that enzymatic cleavage of peptide bonds is challenging and requires highly specialized catalysts. The new research demonstrates AI-designed proteins capable of catalyzing the breakdown of these stubborn peptide bonds, an achievement that could enable new applications in therapeutic protein degradation and synthetic biology.
