### [Google confirms Pixel modem zero-day under targeted exploitation](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/16/google-says-some-pixel-phone-owners-were-hacked-in-zero-day-attacks/) ⭐️ 8.0/10



rss · TechCrunch · Sep 16, 14:47

#### Summary

Google announced that a vulnerability in the modem of some Pixel phones may be under limited, targeted exploitation. The company indicated that certain Pixel phone owners were hacked through a zero-day bug in the device&\#x27;s modem. Google characterized the attacks as targeted rather than widespread, suggesting the vulnerability is being used selectively against specific individuals. No further technical details about the scope of affected models or the patching timeline were provided in the initial report.

#### Background

A modem is the component in a smartphone that handles cellular network connectivity, enabling internet access and communication with cell towers. A zero-day vulnerability refers to a software flaw that is unknown to the vendor and therefore unpatched, making it valuable to attackers who can exploit it before a fix exists. CVE-2026-58704 is the identifier assigned to the specific modem bug in Google Pixel phones that Google confirmed was under targeted exploitation in the wild.

### [Iran strikes on Amazon data centers caused permanent data loss](https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2026/09/iran-strikes-on-amazon-data-centers-caused-permanent-loss-of-customer-data/) ⭐️ 8.0/10



rss · Ars Technica · Sep 16, 16:40

#### Summary

Iranian strikes on Amazon data centers caused permanent loss of customer data, according to an Ars Technica report. The war damage exceeded the threshold AWS services are designed to withstand, exposing critical limitations in cloud infrastructure resilience against conflict-scale attacks. The incident highlights vulnerabilities in AWS&\#x27;s disaster recovery and physical security planning for data centers located in or near conflict zones.

#### Background

AWS \(Amazon Web Services\) operates data centers across the Middle East, including facilities in Bahrain and the United Arab Emirates, which serve customers whose workloads and storage depend on these regions for low-latency access. Under normal circumstances, AWS employs replication, backup, and disaster-recovery architectures designed to survive localized failures; however, these protections presuppose that at least some infrastructure in a given region remains intact. When Iranian drone strikes physically destroyed parts of these data centers, any customer data that was not replicated out-of-region or backed up elsewhere was irreversibly lost. This highlights a fundamental limitation of cloud resilience: disaster recovery is only as strong as the customer&\#x27;s backup strategy, and region-specific deployments without cross-region redundancy are vulnerable to conflict-scale physical damage.

### [1.7M Chinese Casino Sites Hide APT C2 Infrastructure Since 2023](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/09/15/low-quality-casino-sites-conceal-highly-dangerous-threat-actors/5296652) ⭐️ 8.0/10



telegram · zaihuapd · Sep 16, 07:31

#### Summary

Cybersecurity researchers have identified approximately 1.7 million low-quality Chinese-language gambling and adult websites being exploited as covert command-and-control \(C2\) infrastructure by the China-linked APT group PeckBirdy since 2023. The framework conceals C2 domains within these sites, making them nearly indistinguishable from legitimate gambling platforms and causing security analysts to potentially dismiss related traffic as routine employee browsing violations. In addition to C2 hiding, PeckBirdy employs social engineering tactics such as fake software update prompts to lure victims into downloading malware. This pattern represents a convergence of cybercrime-enabling infrastructure and nation-state espionage operations.

### [Micron Shows World&\#x27;s First 512 GB DDR5 RDIMM, Mass Production in 2027](https://videocardz.com/newz/micron-says-worlds-first-512gb-ddr5-module-will-be-production-ready-for-2027) ⭐️ 8.0/10



telegram · zaihuapd · Sep 16, 16:15

#### Summary

Micron has displayed the world&\#x27;s first 512 GB DDR5 RDIMM designed for server applications, operating at speeds up to 9200 MT/s. The module uses 3D-stacked DRAM chips, and 24 units can be combined to deliver 12 TB of memory. Micron claims single-module power consumption of just 16 W, a reduction of over 60% compared to the 44.2 W consumed by four 128 GB modules. AMD and Intel are currently validating the module for future server platforms, with mass production expected in 2027.
