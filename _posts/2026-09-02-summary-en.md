### [Anthropic releases Claude Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-1) ⭐️ 8.0/10



hackernews · denysvitali · Sep 1, 17:53 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49525378)

#### Summary

Anthropic has announced Claude Fable 5.1 and Claude Mythos 5.1, with documented improvements in writing style, instruction adherence, and scientific reasoning capabilities. A key pricing change accompanies the release: cache read pricing for Fable 5.1 dropped from $1/M to $0.25/M, bringing it to roughly half of Opus&\#x27;s cache read cost. Community feedback from an Anthropic employee notes the new model sounds less stereotypically like previous Claude models and responds more reliably to style instructions, while also highlighting significant science improvements. Some users reported a behavioral quirk on complex asynchronous workloads where the model may describe rather than execute next steps unless prompted not to. Additionally, one contributor shared that testing thinking effort levels \(including xhigh and max\) revealed substantial reasoning trace generation at longer durations.

#### Background

Claude Fable and Mythos are mid-tier models in Anthropic&\#x27;s Claude lineup, positioned between lighter and heavier models like Opus. The introduction of variable thinking effort levels \(low, medium, high, xhigh, max\) allows users to trade latency and cost for reasoning depth, a feature Anthropic has been refining across its model family.

#### Community Discussion

An Anthropic employee praised the improved prose quality and more natural writing style in Fable 5.1. Users noted the asynchronous workload behavior quirk and the existence of a mitigation prompt in the docs. Pricing analysis from the community suggested the original Fable pricing limited adoption, and the reduction may signal a broader ceiling on LLM pricing. One user shared detailed reasoning trace visuals from extended thinking effort testing.

#### Summary

Meta has agreed to a $18 billion settlement addressing failures in protecting children on its platforms, including Instagram and Facebook. The settlement represents one of the largest ever paid by a technology company for alleged child safety violations and signals a significant shift in how regulators are holding big tech accountable. It carries major implications for platform regulation, content moderation practices, and how companies deploy AI and machine learning systems to detect and prevent harm to minors online. Legal experts suggest the settlement will reshape industry standards and likely influence future legislative efforts around digital child protection.

#### Background

The settlement arises from allegations that Meta failed to adequately safeguard underage users from harmful content, exploitation, and unsafe interactions on its platforms. Regulators in multiple jurisdictions have increasingly targeted social media companies over child safety, building on existing frameworks such as the Children&\#x27;s Online Privacy Protection Act \(COPPA\) in the United States and the UK&\#x27;s Online Safety Act. The rise of AI-driven content moderation tools has become a focal point, as companies face pressure to demonstrate effective automated detection of abuse while balancing free expression concerns.
