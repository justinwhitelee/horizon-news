### [Stanford study finds brain is two evolutionarily distinct organs](https://www.solidot.org/story?sid=85426) ⭐️ 8.0/10



telegram · zaihuapd · Sep 20, 12:11

#### Summary

A Stanford University School of Medicine study published in Nature reveals that the brain consists of two evolutionarily distinct organs originating from separate progenitor cell lineages, overturning the centuries-old model of unified brain development. Researchers observed developing mouse embryos and identified two non-overlapping progenitor populations: cells expressing the Otx2 gene, which form the forebrain and midbrain, and cells expressing the Gbx2 gene, which form the hindbrain. These two cell groups are mutually exclusive from the earliest stages of development, never overlapping throughout the process. The older, more primitive system governs physiological functions such as heartbeat and breathing, while the newer system underlies uniquely human cognitive abilities including reasoning, mathematics, and poetry.

#### Background

The conventional model of brain development has held that a single progenitor cell population gives rise to the entire brain, implying a common developmental origin for all brain regions. The Otx2 and Gbx2 genes are well-known transcription factors involved in anterior-posterior axis specification during embryonic development, and their mutually exclusive expression domains have been observed in earlier studies, but this work frames them as defining two distinct evolutionary lineages within a single organism.
