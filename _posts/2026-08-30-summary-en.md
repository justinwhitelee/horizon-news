### [Tencent Releases Hy4 Preview: 770B Open-Weight LLM](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/29/hy4/) ⭐️ 8.0/10



rss · Simon Willison · Aug 29, 23:53

#### Summary

Tencent has released Hy4 Preview, an open-weight text-only large language model available on Hugging Face at 1.56TB. The model features 770B total parameters with 49B active parameters and a 1M token context window, representing a substantial jump from the previous Hy3 model which had 295B total parameters, 21B active parameters, and a 256,000 token context. The model supports two reasoning effort levels: &quot;high&quot; \(the default\) and &quot;no\_think&quot; \(disabling chain-of-thought reasoning\). Initial testing by Simon Willison demonstrated the model&\#x27;s SVG generation capability with detailed internal reasoning traces that used truncated, token-efficient English.

### [Sony Music and Warner Chappell sue Anthropic over AI training copyright](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/986438/sony-music-warner-chappell-anthropic-lawsuit-copyright) ⭐️ 8.0/10



rss · The Verge · Aug 29, 18:19

#### Summary

Sony Music and Warner Chappell have filed a copyright lawsuit against Anthropic in the US District Court for the Northern District of California, alleging the AI company used tens of thousands of copyrighted works to train its models without permission. The plaintiffs are seeking up to $150,000 per work in statutory damages, plus an additional up to $25,000 for each instance when identifiable copyright data was stripped from the material. The case centers on whether AI companies can lawfully incorporate copyrighted music and compositions into their training datasets without licensing them.

#### Background

Copyright litigation over AI training data has become a major flashpoint in the technology and creative industries. Statutory damages for willful copyright infringement in the US can reach up to $150,000 per work, which is why the damages sought in this case could amount to hundreds of millions of dollars if the court finds liability.

### [SPC method beats SOTA on TSB-AD benchmark, spurring critique](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1w1wt1s/you_can_beat_sota_time_series_anomaly_detection/) ⭐️ 8.0/10



reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/eamonnkeogh · Aug 29, 20:16

#### Summary

Eamonn Keogh has publicly challenged the validity of the TSB-AD-M benchmark, a widely used evaluation dataset for time series anomaly detection papers at venues like NeurIPS, SIGKDD, and VLDB. Testing the benchmark datasets, Keogh found that a century-old Statistical Process Control \(SPC\) algorithm achieves perfect results on many traces, including several marked as &quot;TAO&quot; \(Trivially Anomalous Objects\). He argues the benchmark is too trivial to make meaningful claims about the proposed algorithms in recent TSAD papers and urges the community to pursue more challenging benchmarks, pointing to his prior work involving sled dogs, tuna, fuel cells, and smart manufacturing data.

#### Background

Eamonn Keogh is a prominent researcher in time series data mining, best known for contributions such as the matrix profile technique and the UCR Time Series Archive. Statistical Process Control \(SPC\) is a classical methodology from industrial engineering, dating to the early 20th century, that detects anomalies in sequential data using control charts and simple statistical thresholds. The TSB-AD benchmark was proposed by Paparrizos and colleagues as a standard evaluation suite for modern TSAD methods.
