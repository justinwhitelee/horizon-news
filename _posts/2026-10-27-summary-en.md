### [OpenAI claims AI-assisted proof refuting the Exponential Time Hypothesis](https://openai.com/index/building-scientific-understanding-with-academic-communities/) ⭐️ 9.0/10



hackernews · jonluca · Oct 26, 21:35 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50251344)

#### Summary

OpenAI has announced that its models produced a natural-language proof refuting the Exponential Time Hypothesis \(ETH\) in computational complexity theory, along with separate results for Yang–Mills existence and quantum chromodynamics. The ETH paper provides a deterministic algorithm for exactly counting satisfying assignments of arbitrary k-CNF formulas that runs in time \(n+m\)^\{O\_k\(1\)\} · 2^\{n/\(log n\)^\{1/2+o\(1\)\}\}, which is sub-exponential but still superpolynomial. OpenAI originally produced the Yang–Mills proof on September 9, 2026 and a QCD proof on September 18, 2026, postponing public release until the Lean formalization of Yang–Mills was completed on October 16, 2026. The ETH being false does not immediately imply P=NP, but it upends a widely used hardness assumption underlying parameterized complexity theory. Community discussion on Hacker News highlights astonishment at long-standing conjectures falling quickly and asks whether the result shifts priors toward P=NP.

#### Background

The Exponential Time Hypothesis, proposed by Impagliazzo, Paturi, and Zane in 2001, posits that 3-SAT cannot be solved in sub-exponential time, meaning no algorithm can decide satisfiability of a 3-CNF formula with n variables in time 2^\{o\(n\)\}. It has become a standard foundational assumption for hardness results in parameterized complexity. The Yang–Mills existence and mass gap problem is one of the Clay Mathematics Institute&\#x27;s seven Millennium Prize Problems, asking whether a quantum Yang–Mills theory exists with a mass gap in four-dimensional spacetime. Lean is a formal proof assistant used to mechanically verify mathematical proofs at machine-checkable rigor.

#### Community Discussion

Hacker News commenters expressed disbelief and excitement at ETH being refuted, noting it was a hypothesis they remembered from university-level complexity theory courses and one that many papers depended on for hardness bounds. Commenters observed that long-standing open questions appear to be resolving rapidly and asked whether the ETH result significantly updates priors toward P=NP, though they acknowledged the two problems are not equivalent. One commenter emphasized the practical timeline: OpenAI produced natural-language proofs in September 2026 and only released after completing formal Lean verification in October 2026.

#### Summary

OpenAI has released GPT-6.1 Terra, a fast, efficient model emphasizing visual reasoning and precise computer use alongside clearer writing. Positioned as a mid-range offering comparable to Anthropic&\#x27;s Sonnet tier, it costs $1 per million input tokens and $5 per million output tokens—approximately half the price of GPT‑5.6 Terra and GPT‑6.1 Sol, yet about ten times more expensive than GPT‑6 Luna. According to OpenAI’s Ted Sanders, Terra excels at technical communication, matches Astra and Sol on vision and computer use while being significantly faster, but trails on other tasks. The model is well-suited for automated, repetitive workloads such as drafting proposals, interpreting technical drawings, filling forms, and editing videos. A demonstration using a “max” reasoning level rendered a pelican riding a bicycle in 4 minutes 11 seconds for $0.113161, illustrating its capability across reasoning tiers.
