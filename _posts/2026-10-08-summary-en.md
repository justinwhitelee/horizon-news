### [Anthropic launches Claude Haiku 5.5 with variable-cost thinking and revised pricing](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-haiku-5-5) ⭐️ 9.0/10



hackernews · sfkgtbor · Oct 7, 18:01 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49996437)

#### Summary

Anthropic has released Claude Haiku 5.5, introducing variable-cost thinking levels that let users trade cost and latency for reasoning depth. The model features revised per-token pricing tiers and new monthly API credits for Max and Team subscribers: Max 5x users receive $100/month, Max 20x users receive $200/month, and Team subscribers can access up to $500 pooled per month. Community reports indicate the cheapest thinking level costs roughly 0.09 cents for a simple task while the highest level can cost over 3 cents, with quality scaling across levels. Independent benchmarks reported by users show Haiku 5.5 is roughly 9x cheaper than Haiku 4.5 and delivers measurably better accuracy on data analytics tasks, at a cost of approximately $0.38 per 40-question exam.

#### Community Discussion

Some users raised concern that the 100k-token pricing cutoff is unusually low and applies only to Haiku, not to Sonnet or Opus, which could cause costs to spike quickly for agent-based workflows. Others highlighted the new monthly credits as a significant benefit that makes it feasible to ship AI-enhanced features under a subscription without relying solely on on-device models. Benchmark reports from independent users show competitive pricing and improved accuracy compared to Haiku 4.5.

#### Summary

A recent Nature article examines non-reciprocity in quantum technologies, highlighting research critical for quantum computing and hardware systems. Non-reciprocity refers to directional signal propagation, where signals travel preferentially in one direction, a key challenge for quantum device design. The publication underscores the importance of this area for advancing quantum engineering and overcoming limitations in quantum circuits. As peer-reviewed research, it provides reliable insights into current developments and practical implications for quantum hardware.

#### Background

Non-reciprocity describes systems where the response differs depending on the direction of excitation. In quantum technologies, achieving non-reciprocity is essential for isolating components and preventing signal interference. This concept arises from classical physics but presents unique challenges at quantum scales due to the need to preserve delicate quantum states.
